AM Floor-Ready Quest

From training room to floor-ready.

A game-based lab for new Account Managers to practice real merchant judgment: what to inspect, what to ask, what to recommend, what to document, and when to slow down before selling.

Purpose: this is not a final exam. It is a practice arena. Every activity uses fictitious merchants and pushes the Account Manager to combine menu optimization, support readiness, Merchant Portal navigation, campaign fit, objection handling, and call quality.

Start

Practice like the merchant will not follow the script.

Use the games to practice realistic merchant judgment through signals, scenarios, and coaching feedback that rewards decision-making over memorization.

For the Account Manager

Use each game to make a decision before checking the feedback. The goal is not to memorize answers; it is to build the mental path you need on live calls.

For Team Leads

Use the report as a coaching conversation starter. It is local practice evidence, not a production scorecard. Pair it with mock calls, quality assurance, shadowing and live performance.

Recommended facilitation: 25 minutes solo practice, 15 minutes pair discussion, 10 minutes Team Lead debrief on one merchant scenario.

Route

Readiness path: from tool lookup to live judgment.

This path trains five behaviors: research fast, diagnose with evidence, guide the merchant, protect trust, and close with clear follow-up.

1. Inspect

Look at the right signal before recommending anything.

2. Discover

Ask the question that reveals the merchant goal or constraint.

3. Decide

Choose the next action that creates value without overpromising.

Game 1

Practice flashcards.

Tap each card. The back side gives the live-call use, not just a definition.

Game 2

Toolbelt memory: find the right place fast.

Match the tool or portal area to the action it supports. Some pairings are intentionally close because floor mistakes often come from using an almost-correct path.

Game 5

SPIN/FAB Forge.

Classify discovery questions and pitch components. The goal is to stop pitching features before the merchant gives you the business problem.

Game 6

Rejection Dojo: handle objections without pushing.

Pick the answer that validates, uses evidence, and lands on a controlled next step.

Game 7

Merchant score cards.

Flip each fictitious merchant card. First decide what you would do; then compare against a floor-ready approach.

Game 8

Campaign Lab: choose the lever, guardrail, and first question.

A campaign recommendation is only strong when it fits the business goal, the merchant constraint, and the readiness of the store page.

Recommendation
Select a goal and context.

The lab will return a recommended lever, the guardrail, and the discovery question.

Game 9

Support Triage Lab.

Not every issue starts with a category. First understand the need, verify facts, check whether something already exists, and document cleanly.

Game 10

Video Gatekeeper.

Choose whether the video should be encouraged, coached before upload, or rejected/escalated. Focus on merchant ownership, quality, and guidelines.

Game 11

Call Flow Builder.

Practice the structure. It is a game, not a scored assessment: move the steps until the conversation protects quality and earns the right to recommend.

Use arrows to move steps. In a real call, the flow is flexible, but the principle is constant: do not sell before you understand the problem.
Game 12
